# Unit 2 formula and constant packet

public-student | All competition levels | Adds F09–F18; F01–F08 remain unchanged.

Values used when specified: c_water=4.18 J/(g·°C); c_ice=2.10 J/(g·°C); L_f,water=334 J/g near 0 °C and 1 atm; L_v,water=2260 J/g near 100 °C and 1 atm. These rounded teaching values depend on phase and conditions. They are not exact universal material constants.

## F09 - Sensible heat
Q=mc(T_f−T_i)

Variables: Q: J; m: g; c: J/(g·°C); T_f,T_i: °C

Use: Single phase; approximately constant c; stated heat model. Positive Q enters sample.

Mini example: 20 g×4.18 J/(g·°C)×5 °C=418 J.

## F10 - Temperature from heat
ΔT=Q/(mc); T_f=T_i+ΔT

Variables: ΔT: °C or K change; Q: J; m: g; c: J/(g·°C)

Use: Find a rise before adding it to the starting reading; verify no phase boundary is crossed.

Mini example: 836 J/(40 g×4.18 J/(g·°C))=5 °C; from 19 °C gives 24 °C.

## F11 - Transition heat and partial transition
|Q|=mL; m_changed=|Q|/L

Variables: Q: J; m: g; L: J/g

Use: Use the correct transition and conditions. Heating transitions are positive for sample; reverse transitions negative.

Mini example: 2 g×334 J/g=668 J to melt ice at its melting point.

## F12 - Stage-by-stage energy
Q_total=Q_1+Q_2+…

Variables: Q_i: J per stage

Use: Separate warming/cooling from each phase change. Use a different c for each phase when needed.

Mini example: Ice warming 42 J + melting 668 J + liquid warming 83.6 J =793.6 J.

## F13 - Energy at constant net input
Q=P_netΔt

Variables: Q: J; P_net: W=J/s; Δt: s

Use: P_net is the stated transfer into the sample, not automatically a lamp’s electrical rating.

Mini example: 50 W×120 s=6000 J.

## F14 - Average graph slope
rate=(T_2−T_1)/(t_2−t_1)

Variables: T: °C; t: min; rate: °C/min

Use: Read both axes; a cooling rate is negative under final-minus-initial convention.

Mini example: (40−20) °C/(6−2) min=5 °C/min.

## F15 - Ideal gas law
PV=nRT

Variables: P: absolute kPa; V: L; n: mol; T: K; R: 8.314 kPa·L/(mol·K)

Use: Ideal-gas approximation; Kelvin and absolute pressure. Alternative matched sets use Pa/m³ or atm/L with matching R.

Mini example: 0.100 mol×8.314×300 K / 2.00 L=124.71 kPa≈125 kPa.

## F16 - Mass to moles
n=m/M

Variables: n: mol; m: g; M: g/mol

Use: M is supplied molar mass; do not substitute grams for moles.

Mini example: 6.00 g/(12.0 g/mol)=0.500 mol.

## F17 - Two ideal-gas states
P_1V_1/T_1=P_2V_2/T_2

Variables: P_1,P_2: same absolute-pressure unit; V_1,V_2: same volume unit; T_1,T_2: K

Use: Requires constant n and ideal behavior; simplify only quantities explicitly fixed.

Mini example: At fixed n,V: 100 kPa×330 K/300 K=110 kPa.

## F18 - Gas-law unit and pressure conversions
1 kPa=1000 Pa; 1 L=0.001 m³; 1 atm=101.325 kPa; P_abs=P_gauge+P_atm

Variables: P_abs,P_gauge,P_atm: same pressure unit

Use: Pressure in PV=nRT is relative to vacuum. 1 kPa·L=1 Pa·m³=1 J.

Mini example: A 15 kPa gauge reading with local atmosphere 99 kPa gives 114 kPa absolute.
