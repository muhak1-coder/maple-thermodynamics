# Unit 2 glossary

public-student | All levels | Complements the unchanged Unit 1 glossary.

**Absolute pressure:** Pressure measured relative to a vacuum; the pressure required by PV=nRT.

**Boiling:** Vapor bubbles form and grow within a liquid when vapor pressure reaches surrounding pressure under the stated conditions.

**Condensation:** Gas-to-liquid transition; releases heat from the condensing sample under the usual thermal transition model.

**Critical point:** Endpoint of the liquid-gas coexistence curve, beyond which the ordinary liquid-gas distinction disappears.

**Deposition:** Direct gas-to-solid transition.

**Endothermic phase change:** Transition that absorbs heat into the selected sample, such as melting.

**Evaporation:** Liquid-to-gas transfer at the surface; can occur below boiling temperature.

**Exothermic phase change:** Transition that releases heat from the selected sample, such as freezing.

**Freezing:** Liquid-to-solid transition.

**Fusion:** Another name for melting in latent-heat terminology; not nuclear fusion here.

**Gauge pressure:** Pressure measured relative to the surrounding atmosphere.

**Ideal gas:** Model with negligible particle volume and negligible interactions except ideal collisions.

**Latent heat:** Transition heat per mass at specified conditions, usually J/g or J/kg.

**Melting:** Solid-to-liquid transition.

**Molar mass:** Mass per mole of specified entities, often g/mol.

**Mole:** Amount containing exactly 6.02214076×10²³ specified entities.

**Net heating power:** Effective rate of heat input into the sample; not necessarily the source power rating.

**Phase boundary:** Pressure-temperature conditions where adjacent phases can coexist in equilibrium.

**Phase diagram:** Map of stable equilibrium phases as a function of conditions, usually pressure and temperature.

**Plateau:** Approximately horizontal temperature segment; an ideal phase transition can produce one at fixed pressure.

**Sensible heat:** Heat associated with a temperature change in the specified model.

**Specific heat:** Heat per mass per temperature change under specified conditions.

**Sublimation:** Direct solid-to-gas transition.

**Supercritical fluid:** Fluid above the critical temperature and pressure without a distinct ordinary liquid-gas boundary.

**Supercooling:** A liquid remaining liquid temporarily below its equilibrium freezing temperature.

**Triple point:** Particular temperature and pressure permitting solid, liquid and gas coexistence in equilibrium.

**Vaporization:** Liquid-to-gas transition; includes evaporation and boiling.

**Vapor pressure:** Equilibrium pressure of a substance’s vapor above its condensed phase at a given temperature.
