# Unit 5 historical evidence and source guide

public-student | Checked 2026-10-18 UTC | Original instructional writing

## How evidence is used
Historical sources establish dates, attributions and documented relationships. The physical examples, numerical data, diagrams and classroom scenarios are original educational models, not claimed historical measurements. No historical quotations are invented or reproduced. This unit is a teaching synthesis, not an exhaustive scholarly history. Primary texts retain obsolete terminology; modern explanations explicitly translate it. University histories, museum collections, Nobel archives and the SI authority provide context or verification.

## H01
University of Edinburgh, School of Chemistry. Joseph Black (1728–1799).

https://chem.ed.ac.uk/about-us/history/professors/joseph-black

Life dates; Glasgow investigations; 1762 presentation. Institutional history.

## H02
Joseph Black. Lectures on the Elements of Chemistry, edited posthumously by John Robison (1803). Excerpts hosted by Le Moyne College, from Magie’s Source Book.

https://webserver.lemoyne.edu/giunta/blackheat.html

Primary-source excerpts: distinction between temperature and thermal quantity, material heat capacities and melting observations. Old terminology is translated, not adopted as modern physics.

## H03
MacTutor, University of St Andrews. William Thomson (1824–1907).

https://mathshistory.st-andrews.ac.uk/Biographies/Thomson/

Biographical context; 1848 temperature-scale proposal; development of thermodynamics and Joule collaboration.

## H04
BIPM. SI base unit: kelvin.

https://www.bipm.org/en/si-base-units/kelvin

Authoritative modern definition and exact Boltzmann constant, 1.380649×10⁻²³ J/K. Not a description of Kelvin’s 1848 definition.

## Attribution limits
The eight required scientists were not the only contributors to thermodynamics. A formula associated with a scientist may have modern notation or later refinements. Carnot’s 1824 caloric setting is distinguished from modern conservation and the Kelvin-based bound. Thomson’s first absolute-scale proposal is distinguished from the current SI definition. Clausius’s naming of entropy is distinguished from Boltzmann’s statistical interpretation. Planck’s energy elements are distinguished from Einstein’s later light-quantum proposal. Maxwell–Boltzmann is not treated as proof of a coauthored paper.

## Rules scope
The supplied 2027 Division B manual, printed B57, 5.c.v lists all eight historical contributors. This unit provides all-level historical context. Detailed blackbody, radiant-exitance, Stefan–Boltzmann and third-law content is separately State/National-only under 5.c.vi and remains Unit 6. Exercises follow 5.d: arithmetic, ratios, exponents, one-variable algebra and elementary counting. No logarithmic entropy calculation, calculus, trigonometry or advanced probability is required. The protected manual is not redistributed. No claim is made that later rule clarifications have been checked.
