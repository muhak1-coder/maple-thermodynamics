# Unit 1 glossary

public-student | Full seven-unit glossary pending.

**Boundary**: The real or imaginary surface separating a chosen system from its surroundings.

**Calorie (Cal)**: A food-label energy unit usually equal to 1 kilocalorie, or 4184 J in this packet.

**calorie (cal)**: The small thermochemical energy unit defined here as 4.184 J.

**Closed system**: A system that can exchange energy but not matter with its surroundings.

**Density**: Mass per volume, ρ=m/V; an intensive property at specified conditions.

**Energy**: A conserved physical quantity transferred by heating, work and other routes; measured in joules.

**Extensive property**: A property that scales with the amount of a system in a controlled same-state comparison.

**Heat**: Energy transferred because of a temperature difference; a transfer, not stored material.

**Homogeneous**: Uniform in composition and relevant properties on the scale being considered.

**Intensive property**: A property that does not simply scale with the amount of a same-state sample.

**Internal energy**: Microscopic energy associated with a system’s particles and interactions, excluding whole-object motion and gravitational energy.

**Isolated system**: An ideal system exchanging neither energy nor matter with its surroundings.

**Joule (J)**: The SI unit of energy.

**Kelvin (K)**: The absolute-temperature unit with the same step size as a Celsius degree.

**Kilojoule (kJ)**: 1000 joules.

**Net transfer**: Transfer in one direction minus transfer in the opposite direction.

**Open system**: A system that can exchange matter and energy with its surroundings.

**Power**: Energy transfer or conversion per unit time; measured in watts.

**Surroundings**: Everything outside the chosen system boundary.

**System**: The selected matter or region studied in a problem.

**Temperature**: A thermal-state quantity that determines the direction of spontaneous net heat transfer.

**Temperature change**: Final temperature minus initial temperature; offsets cancel in scale conversions.

**Thermal energy**: A classroom term for temperature-related microscopic energy; usage varies, so use internal energy in precise balances.

**Thermal equilibrium**: Equal-temperature condition giving no net heat transfer between bodies in thermal contact.

**Watt (W)**: One joule per second, a unit of power.

**Watt-hour (Wh)**: An energy unit equal to 3600 J.

**Zeroth law**: If two systems are each in thermal equilibrium with a third, they are in thermal equilibrium with each other.
