# Unit 5 common confusions reference

public-student | All competition levels

| Confusion | Correction and reason |
| --- | --- |
| Kelvin and Black both discovered heat. | Kelvin developed absolute-temperature ideas; Black clarified specific and latent heat. Neither broad phrase captures their separate contributions. |
| Same temperature means the same thermal energy. | Mass, material and phase matter. A thermometer alone does not provide an energy total. |
| A melting plateau means no heat enters. | Energy can drive a phase change with little temperature change. |
| Joule’s paddles supply heat through the shaft. | For the water system the shaft supplies work. Warming describes the result, not the transfer mode. |
| Carnot wrote today’s complete energy theory in 1824. | His published framework used caloric assumptions; later thermodynamics revised the energy interpretation. |
| Any engine below 100% is feasible. | It must also respect the reservoir-dependent Carnot bound and other physical constraints. |
| Clausius and Boltzmann both first named entropy. | Clausius named entropy in 1865; Boltzmann developed its statistical interpretation, notably in 1877. |
| Entropy is energy that was destroyed. | Entropy is a state property in J/K. Energy is conserved even when useful work potential decreases. |
| Every molecule at one temperature has the same speed. | Temperature describes collective behavior; equilibrium gases have distributions. |
| Maxwell–Boltzmann proves a shared paper. | The name recognizes connected contributions and is not evidence of coauthorship. |
| The demon is a proven perpetual-motion machine. | It is a thought experiment. Include the physical controller and repeated restoration in the full accounting. |
| Planck invented the word entropy. | His key contribution here concerns radiation and energy quanta; Clausius introduced entropy. |
| 1900 and 1918 are interchangeable Planck dates. | 1900 labels the breakthrough; 1918 labels the Nobel Prize. |
| Blackbody means no emission. | It is an ideal absorber and thermal emitter; sufficiently hot examples glow. |
| A diagram proves historical collaboration. | A conceptual map can show related ideas without documenting personal interaction. |
