# Unit 1 review sheet

public-student | All levels | Use after WS01–WS06.

## Six ideas to retrieve without notes
1. Temperature is a thermal-state quantity; internal energy belongs to a system; heat is an energy transfer caused by a temperature difference.
2. Choose a boundary before classifying a system. Open exchanges matter and energy; closed exchanges energy but not matter; ideal isolated exchanges neither.
3. Compare portions at the same state: mass, volume and internal energy are extensive; temperature, pressure and density are intensive.
4. Thermal equilibrium means equal temperature and no net heat transfer, not no particle motion. A common reference connects temperature comparisons through the zeroth law.
5. Reading conversions use offsets. Temperature-change conversions do not. Kelvin has no degree symbol.
6. J and kJ measure energy; W measures power. Use seconds in E=Pt when P is in W. Keep cal and food Cal distinct.

## A five-step problem routine
1. Name the system and requested quantity.
2. Write the given values with units; say whether temperature is a reading or change.
3. Select a relationship and state any assumption.
4. Substitute consistent units, calculate, and round once at the end.
5. Interpret the sign and size. Does the answer describe energy, power or temperature?

## Retrieval tasks
Explain the spoon/bucket comparison. Draw a closed-system energy arrow. Split a sample and name changing properties. Explain a thermometer. Convert readings, changes and energy units.

## Error checks before moving on
If you wrote “heat inside the cup,” revisit WS01. If a seal made you say isolated, revisit WS02. If two 20 °C samples became 40 °C, revisit WS03. If equilibrium meant equal energy, revisit WS04. If you added 273.15 to a rise, revisit WS05. If minutes went directly into W×time or Cal meant cal, revisit WS06.
