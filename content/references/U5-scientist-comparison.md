# Unit 5 scientist comparison chart

public-student | All competition levels

The equations are modern associations, not a claim that each individual wrote the modern formula alone. Life dates provide context; contribution dates belong in the timeline.

| Scientist and lifespan | Contribution | Modern concept and significance |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Joseph Black (1728–1799) | Specific heat; latent heat | Separated temperature from thermal quantity; foundation for calorimetry. Q=mcΔT; Q=mL (modern notation) [H01,H02] |
| Sadi Carnot (1796–1832) | Ideal reversible engine reasoning | Established roots of a general performance limit; original caloric framework later revised. Modern η_max=1−T_C/T_H [H06] |
| James Prescott Joule (1818–1889) | Mechanical equivalent of heat | Quantitative experimental support for energy conversion and conservation. ΔU=Q−W; joule J [H05] |
| Rudolf Clausius (1822–1888) | Second-law development; entropy | Added direction and irreversibility to energy accounting. Total isolated entropy cannot decrease [H07] |
| William Thomson / Lord Kelvin (1824–1907) | Absolute temperature; thermodynamic theory | Developed a general temperature framework; original proposal later refined. T=t+273.15; Kelvin ratios [H03,H04] |
| James Clerk Maxwell (1831–1879) | Molecular velocity distribution; demon | Connected particle populations with bulk properties; examined second-law assumptions. Statistical gas description [H10,H11,H14] |
| Ludwig Boltzmann (1844–1906) | Statistical interpretation of entropy | Explained thermal regularities through microscopic possibilities. Multiplicity; k_B links energy and K [H12,H13,H04] |
| Max Planck (1858–1947) | Radiation law; energy quanta | Opened quantum theory through the thermal-radiation problem. ε=hf conceptually [H08,H09] |

## Three attribution checks
Clausius named entropy; Boltzmann developed its statistical interpretation. Kelvin’s scale concerns absolute temperature; Black’s latent heat concerns energy during phase change. Joule’s conservation evidence and Carnot’s ideal limits answer different questions.
